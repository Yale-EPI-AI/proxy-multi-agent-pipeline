Summary Research Agent

Average yield of cereals in kg per hectare of harvested land. A measure of agricultural technological intensity — the degree to which modern, input-intensive farming is practiced.

Mechanism Research Agent

High cereal yields reflect modern, organised agricultural systems that: (1) generate large volumes of crop residues that can be composted or digested; (2) use precision logistics and storage infrastructure transferable to waste collection; (3) indicate a technically capable farming sector familiar with organic matter management. Countries with industrialised agriculture also tend to have municipal composting infrastructure for organic household waste, contributing directly to the COM component of WRR. The causal chain runs: agricultural modernisation → institutional capacity and logistics → formal waste management systems.

Share of electricity generated from oil, gas, and coal sources. A measure of the fossil-fuel dependence of the electricity grid and, inversely, the diversification toward alternatives including waste-to-energy.

Mechanism Research Agent

Countries that generate a large share of electricity from fossil fuels have less incentive to develop waste-to-energy plants, because cheap fossil fuels undercut the economics of incineration with energy recovery (INE). Fossil-fuel-heavy grids also tend to be in economies with less developed waste-management infrastructure overall. This provides a novel, sector-specific signal beyond aggregate GDP.
